AI-integrated FLIM System

Challenges of tumour resection

Cancer is a life-threatening disease that is very difficult to treat because of the recurrence after surgical resection. Cancer surgeries are challenging because a ‘complete’ resection may risk damaging surrounding tissue and affecting organ function, whereas a safer and more cautious approach is often followed by tumour recurrence, so patients must undergo follow-up treatments like chemotherapy or second stage surgery (22). More fundamentally, tumour boundaries are often indistinguishable to the naked eye, and smaller lesions can easily remain obscured during surgery. These factors all contribute to the high recurrence rate of cancer.

Current solution: image-guided surgery

Several imaging modalities are currently available to provide intraoperative surgical guidance, including ultrasound, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT) and fluorescence imaging. Although these tools are in use for tumour delineation, most of them are constrained by the following clinical limitations (23, 24):

  • Sophisticated modalities like MRI and CT require specialized technologists
  • Maintenance of MRI and CT scanners are expensive, which limits their accessibility
  • Guidance provided by images is very limited, the determination of resection margin is highly dependent on surgeon’s experience and interpretation
  • Intraoperative scanning and subsequent image processing are time-consuming, so the guidance is technically not in real time
  • Most images are qualitative as they rely on signal intensity rather than absolute biochemical measurements

Overview of AFS

To address the shortcomings of conventional image-guided surgery, we propose the AI-integrated FLIM System (AFS) as the state-of-the-art tool for tumour delineation. AFS combines high-speed FLIM instrument with a centralised online database and a cloud-based artificial intelligence engine to transform fluorescence lifetime data into annotated images and meaningful parameters.

Principle of AI-integrated FLIM system

AFS combines fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy (FLIM) with a flexible endoscope to provide rapid in vivo inspection of human tissue.

AFS is based on time-correlated single photon counting (TCSPC). It utilises pulsed laser as excitation source and single photon avalanche diode (SPAD) sensors to capture spatially resolved, time-resolved fluorescence signals.

The time taken for detection of individual photons after excitation (t) is recorded at a high repetition rate.

The t values at each pixel compiled by our software into a histogram of photon counts versus time. The average fluorescence lifetime is calculated for each pixel to generate a pseudo-coloured lifetime map (i.e. FLIM image).

The lifetime map and the array of histograms are automatically sent to our cloud server for further analysis. Firstly, the lifetime map is analysed by our AI model, which marks the boundary of tumour tissue and give immediate feedback to the end user. Meanwhile, the histograms are enhanced by AI and fitted with exponential decay to extract the fluorescence lifetime components (τ) and the relative populations (α).

Values of τ and α are matched to corresponding autofluorescence molecules based on known lifetime ranges, including NAD(P)H, FAD and collagen. Our AI model analyses the fluorescence lifetime image together with proportions among matched α values to build a probability map of cancer and annotate positive and negative regions of tumour with confidence levels.

Features and advantages

  • The data acquisition time is adjustable to balance resolution and imaging speed
  • Our AI model is trained by machine learning, primarily based on convolutional neural network, which is excellent for pattern recognition (22)
  • The outputs of our AI model are stored in our database and constantly reviewed by our experts for optimisation of the system
